## 📌 Project Overview
The **Zepto Grocery Data Analysis & Business Intelligence Dashboard** project simulates a real-world retail analytics lifecycle. In modern grocery retail, understanding item-level sales drivers, customer preferences across fat contents and product types, and outlet performance across geographical tiers is critical for strategic inventory and marketing decisions. This project ingests raw grocery dataset files, cleans and transforms them through robust pipelines, extracts deep insights via structured SQL queries, and visualizes KPIs in a high-impact Power BI dashboard.

---

## 📦 Dataset Description
The dataset consists of grocery retail records capturing various attributes of items, outlets, and sales performance:
* **`Item Identifier`**: Unique product code.
* **`Item Weight`**: The physical weight of the product.
* **`Item Fat Content`**: Indicates whether the product is Low Fat or Regular.
* **`Item Visibility`**: The percentage of total display area allocated to this product in the store.
* **`Item Type`**: The broad category to which the product belongs (e.g., Fruits and Vegetables, Snack Foods, Dairy, Canned).
* **`Outlet Identifier`**: Unique store identification code.
* **`Outlet Establishment Year`**: The year the store was established.
* **`Outlet Size`**: The spatial capacity of the store (Small, Medium, High).
* **`Outlet Location Type`**: The tier classification of the city where the store is located (Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3).
* **`Outlet Type`**: The operational format of the store (e.g., Supermarket Type1/Type2/Type3, Grocery Store).
* **`Sales`**: Total revenue generated by the item at the specific outlet.
* **`Rating`**: Customer satisfaction and product quality rating (scored up to 5.0).

## 🛠️ End-to-End Project Workflow

### Phase 1: Data Cleaning & Preprocessing (Excel Copilot & Python/Pandas)
* **Initial Inspection (Excel & Copilot):** We loaded the raw spreadsheet into Microsoft Excel to perform preliminary quality checks. AI-assisted inspection via Copilot helped quickly spot inconsistent category naming conventions (e.g., variants like "low fat", "Low Fat", and "LF" mapped together) and evaluate missing values in `Item Weight` and `Outlet Size`.
* **Programmatic Cleansing (Python/Pandas):** 
  * Imported data into Jupyter Notebooks using Pandas.
  * Checked dataset dimensions (8,523 rows and 12 columns) and eliminated duplicate rows.
  * Imputed missing values for continuous variables (`Item Weight`) using appropriate statistical measures (median/mean grouping by item types) and handled categorical nulls (`Outlet Size`) using mode imputation.
  * Standardized categorical string fields to ensure clean aggregation during SQL and Power BI modeling.

### Phase 2: Exploratory Data Analysis & SQL Querying
After loading the clean dataset into a relational database structure, we wrote advanced SQL scripts to answer critical business questions:
1. **Outlet Revenue Contribution:** Evaluated total and average sales across different `Outlet Type` categories to determine which store formats drive maximum gross merchandise value (GMV).
2. **Product Category Analysis:** Identified top-performing product categories (`Item Type`) by total sales volume and average customer ratings.
3. **Consumer Preferences by Fat Content:** Analyzed whether consumer spending trends favor Low Fat or Regular items across different store tiers.

### Phase 3: Power BI Dashboard Development
* Imported the final transformed dataset into Power BI Desktop (`.pbix`).
* **Data Modeling:** Established correct relationships, created calculated measures using DAX (Data Analysis Expressions) for Total Sales, Average Rating, and Total Item Count.
* **Dashboard Layout & Design:** 
  * Designed clean, executive-ready KPI cards at the top for instant visibility into core metrics.
  * Created dynamic bar charts comparing sales across `Item Type` and `Outlet Location Type`.
  * Implemented interactive slicers enabling users to filter data seamlessly by city tiers, outlet establishment years, and item categories.

## 💡 Key Business Insights
* **Supermarket Dominance:** Supermarket Type1 and Type3 formats generate disproportionately higher revenues compared to traditional grocery stores, driven by wider product assortment and larger physical footprints.
* **Category Leaders:** *Fruits and Vegetables* and *Snack Foods* lead overall sales volumes, indicating consistent, high-frequency consumer demand.
* **Geographical Tier Trends:** Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities show immense growth potential, contributing significantly to overall grocery turnover despite lower initial store infrastructure density compared to Tier 1 cities.
* **Customer Ratings Consistency:** Customer ratings remain remarkably stable across product types (averaging near upper bounds), showing high overall satisfaction with product quality.
